● Fine-tuning in one sentence

  Fine-tuning means continuing to train an existing model on your own examples,
  so its weights change. The knowledge or behavior gets baked into the model
  itself.

  RAG leaves the model's weights frozen. At question time, it looks up relevant
  text and pastes it into the prompt.

  Analogy: fine-tuning is studying for a closed-book exam: you memorize, then
  answer from memory. RAG is an open-book exam: you don't memorize anything, you
  just know how to find the right page fast.

  What fine-tuning looks like in practice

  With OpenAI, you upload a file of example conversations (hundreds to
  thousands):
  {"messages": [{"role":"user","content":"What did Eyring teach about peace?"},
                {"role":"assistant","content":"He taught that..."}]}
  OpenAI trains a copy of a model on them and gives you a new model ID to call.
  You never touch the weights yourself.

  The key surprise: fine-tuning is good at teaching how to respond (tone,
  format, style, a narrow task) and unreliable at teaching what is true. A model
  fine-tuned on talks absorbs their "vibe" but will still misquote and invent
  talks, because the facts get blended together rather than stored.

  Trade-offs, applied to your app

  When fine-tuning wins

  - Style or format: e.g. always answering in a strict JSON schema, or in a

  - A narrow, repeated task where a small fine-tuned model replaces a big
    expensive one (e.g. classifying support tickets).
  - Domain jargon the base model handles badly.

  Often both are combined: fine-tune for behavior, use RAG for facts.

  ▎ built into the model. RAG leaves the model unchanged and retrieves relevant
  ▎ text at question time, then puts it in the prompt. Fine-tuning is good at
  ▎ changing how a model responds (tone, format, a narrow task) but unreliable
  ▎ for teaching facts: knowledge gets blended into the weights, so the model
  ▎ can't cite where something came from and may invent quotes. RAG was the
  ▎ right choice here because the goal is knowledge, not behavior. Users need
  ▎ what specific speakers actually said, with the talk named as the source.
  ▎ It's also far cheaper to maintain: when a new conference happens every April
  ▎ and October, I scrape the talks, embed them (embedding all 26,000 sentences
  ▎ cost about $0.02), and insert the rows, with no retraining. RAG also keeps
  ▎ access control in the database: RLS decides what each user can retrieve,
  ▎ while anything trained into a model is available to every user. Fine-tuning
  ▎ would be the better choice for teaching consistent behavior, such as always
  ▎ answering in a fixed format or voice, or training a small, cheap model on a
  ▎ narrow repeated task like classifying questions by topic. The two are often
  ▎ combined: fine-tuning for behavior, RAG for facts.
